# Genre Rules

Jump to the section you need. Each section: **purpose → structure → do → don't**.
Genre rules override general rules where they conflict.

---

## 1. Research paper / skripsi / jurnal

**Purpose:** let a skeptical expert verify the claim.
**Structure:** follow the required template (IMRaD, or the kampus guide). Do not reorder.

Do:
- Keep every citation exactly as given. Never add a reference that was not supplied.
- Keep terms consistent: once "responden", always "responden".
- Passive or impersonal voice is acceptable where the institution expects it ("Data dikumpulkan melalui ...").
- "Signifikan" only for statistical significance, with the test/p-value present.
- Hedge accurately: "menunjukkan", "mengindikasikan" — match the strength of the evidence.
- Paraphrasing a source: change structure and wording, keep meaning, **keep the citation**.

Don't:
- Flowery openings in Pendahuluan ("Di era globalisasi ...").
- Claims in Pembahasan not supported by Hasil.
- Rhetorical questions, emoji, marketing adjectives.

## 2. Report / laporan (kantor, KKN, magang, proyek)

**Purpose:** help a busy reader decide or act.
**Structure:** summary first (what happened, what it means, what's needed) → details → appendix.

Do: numbers with units and periods ("Q3 2026", "Rp12,4 juta"), tables for comparisons, owners and dates on action items.
Don't: chronological narrative when the reader needs outcomes; "kegiatan berjalan lancar dan sukses" without evidence.

## 3. Proposal (bisnis, hibah, kegiatan)

**Purpose:** get a yes.
**Structure:** problem (with evidence) → solution → why you → plan & timeline → budget → expected outcome (measurable).

Do: one sentence the reviewer can repeat; measurable targets; budget that matches the plan.
Don't: "solusi inovatif dan komprehensif"; outcomes like "meningkatkan kesadaran" with no metric.

## 4. Website copy / landing page

**Purpose:** a visitor understands what it is, who it's for, and what to do — in 5 seconds.
**Structure:** headline (what + for whom or outcome) → subhead (how) → proof → features as benefits → objections → CTA.

Do:
- Headline under ~10 words, concrete. "Invoice terkirim otomatis tiap tanggal 1" beats "Solusi Keuangan Masa Depan".
- CTA verb + object: "Coba 14 hari gratis", "Lihat harga".
- Microcopy (buttons, errors, empty states) short and specific: "Email belum terdaftar" not "Terjadi kesalahan".
Don't: invented testimonials, logos, or stats; "Welcome to our website"; every section starting with a question.

## 5. Code: README, docs, comments, commits, PR descriptions, UI strings

**Purpose:** someone gets unblocked fast.

- **README:** one-line what it is → install → minimal usage example → config → license. Real commands that run.
- **Docs:** task-oriented headings ("Menghubungkan database"), steps numbered, one action per step, show expected output.
- **Code comments:** explain *why*, not *what*. Match the density of surrounding code. No "This function is responsible for ...".
- **Commit messages:** imperative, ≤ 50 char subject ("Fix null check in invoice parser"), body only for why.
- **PR descriptions:** what changed, why, how tested, risk. No marketing.
- **Error messages:** what happened + what to do. "File melebihi 10 MB. Kompres atau pilih file lain."
Don't: "robust", "seamless", "powerful" in docs; emoji in commit logs unless the repo convention uses them.

## 6. Video script (YouTube, Reels/TikTok, explainer, ads)

**Purpose:** be heard, not read.

Do:
- Write for the ear: short sentences, contractions/spoken forms, one idea per line.
- Hook in the first 2–3 seconds with the concrete payoff, not "Halo guys, balik lagi di channel ...".
- Two columns if visual matters: VISUAL | AUDIO.
- Read it aloud mentally; cut anything you'd stumble on.
- Mark on-screen text separately; keep it under ~7 words per card.
Don't: long subordinate clauses, parentheses, citations spoken aloud, lists of more than 3 things in voice-over.

## 7. Marketing: ads, social captions, email campaigns

Do:
- One message per piece. One CTA.
- Lead with the reader's situation or the outcome.
- Specific offer terms: price, deadline, what's included.
- Captions: first line must work alone (it's what shows before "more").
- Email subject under ~45 characters, no clickbait the body doesn't pay off.
Don't: "Jangan sampai ketinggalan!!!", stacked hashtags in the body, exclamation marks in series, claims regulated by law (health, finance, "terbaik No.1") without proof.

## 8. Brochure / pamphlet / flyer / poster

**Purpose:** scanned in seconds, often in hand or on a wall.

Do:
- Headline readable from a distance: under ~8 words.
- Hierarchy: headline → 3–5 key facts → contact/CTA. Everything else is optional.
- Practical info exact and complete: date, time, place, price, contact, registration link/QR.
- Brochure panels: each panel stands on its own.
Don't: paragraphs over ~40 words; decorative adjectives competing with the facts.

## 9. Email and messages (bisnis, akademik, customer service)

Do: subject says the ask; first sentence says why you're writing; one clear request with a deadline; sign-off matches register.
Academic email to dosen: sapaan ("Yth. Bapak/Ibu ..."), perkenalan singkat (nama, NIM, kelas), keperluan, terima kasih.
Don't: three paragraphs of pleasantries before the point.

## 10. Speech / sambutan / presentation slides

- Speech: spoken rhythm like video scripts; protocol greeting kept short unless the event requires full protocol.
- Slides: one claim per slide as the title ("Biaya kirim turun 22% setelah rute baru"), not a topic label ("Hasil").

## 11. CV, cover letter, LinkedIn, bio

Do: action verb + result + number ("Memangkas waktu rekap dari 2 hari ke 3 jam"). Bio in third person for events, first person for profiles.
Don't: "hardworking, passionate, detail-oriented team player".

## 12. Legal-adjacent text (terms, privacy notice, policy)

Only simplify wording; never change obligations, scope, or numbers. Flag to the user that legal text needs review by a qualified person.
